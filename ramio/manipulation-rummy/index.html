Manipulation Rummy: the rules

How many cards, when you draw, how the joker works: the common rules and Ramio's, side by side.

Solve a puzzle now in your browser

Manipulation rummy is the family of rummy games where you don't just lay down your own melds: on your turn you can take apart and rebuild everything on the table, your opponents' melds included, until your cards fit in. The first player to get rid of all their cards wins.

There is no official rulebook. The game goes by many names (Carousel, Vatikan, Shanghai, Machiavelli, Finagle...), and each version deals a different number of cards and has its own way of drawing, opening and using the joker. So at each point, this page gives two answers side by side: the common rules, as described by card game references such as pagat.com, and how it is played in Ramio, the free app for Android and iPhone where you can play manipulation rummy online, solo or with friends. Ramio's rules are one variant among others, not "the" rules: if you play with real cards, agree on them before you deal.

If you know Rummikub, you will feel at home: the idea is the same, with cards instead of tiles.

In a private room, whoever creates the game can set the deal anywhere from 7 to 15 cards each: enough to find the 7, 10 or 13 cards of your usual table. The remaining cards form the stock, face down. No card is turned up and there is no discard pile. The first player of the first round is drawn at random; after that, the first turn moves on with each round.

Runs and sets: which melds are valid?

  • Run (or sequence): at least three consecutive cards of the same suit, for example 7, 8 and 9 of hearts.
  • Set (or group): three or four cards of the same rank, all of different suits. With two decks you can hold two 9s of spades: they can't go in the same set.

Set 9 9 9
Four K K K K

The ace depends on the version: high only in Finagle, low only in Shanghai, at either end in Carousel. Ramio does the same as Carousel: the ace goes before the 2 (A-2-3) or after the king (Q-K-A), but a run never wraps around: K-A-2 is not allowed.

Do you draw every turn? Do you discard?

This is the question players argue about most, and the versions give different answers.

Common rules

  • In Machiavelli and Vatikan, on your turn you do one thing or the other: either you lay down at least one card, or you draw one card from the stock and pass.
  • In Shanghai and Finagle, you must play if you can; if you can't, you draw cards one at a time until you can.
  • In Carousel, you draw one or two cards and may then meld, or you draw three cards and your turn ends.
  • In all of them, there is no discard pile.

How Ramio plays

  • You always draw one card at the start of your turn: in the app, the draw is automatic.
  • Then you may meld, if you want to and if you can. Melding is optional: you can also pass.
  • You never discard: your hand only shrinks when you lay cards down.

The difference matters. With a draw every turn, you receive a card even when you meld: your hand empties more slowly, and the cards left at the end of the round cost points, but you have more material to rebuild the table with.

How do you open? Is there a minimum?

Common rules

Most versions have no opening requirement: you can lay down any valid meld and work on the table from your first turn. Vatikan is the main exception: your first meld must be a run of three cards in the same suit. Some tables borrow a points threshold, often 30 or 40, from other rummy games.

How Ramio plays

No minimum score, but one condition: your first meld must contain a clean run of three, that is three consecutive cards of the same suit, without a joker, all from your hand. In that same turn you can already extend it (even with a joker), lay down other melds and rearrange the table.

How do you rearrange the table?

This is the heart of the game. On your turn you can move, split and recombine everyone's melds to fit your cards in (in Ramio, once you have opened). Most versions set two conditions: add at least one card from your hand, and leave only valid melds. Ramio checks three:

  • at the end of your turn, the whole table must be valid again: every meld is a run, a set or a four; otherwise everything goes back where it was;
  • your play must use at least one card from your hand;
  • a card on the table never goes back to a hand.

How does the joker work in manipulation rummy?

With or without jokers? Both are played, and this is where the rules vary most.

Common rules

  • How many: Machiavelli, Shanghai and Finagle are played without jokers; Vatikan uses two, Carousel one or two.
  • Moving it: usually, a joker on the table only comes free when someone puts the real card it stands for in its place. In Vatikan it can't move from one meld to another otherwise; in Carousel, the freed joker must go straight into a new meld.
  • Taking it back: some home rules let the player who brings the real card keep the joker. Agree on it before you start.
  • Left in hand: its value depends on the version, 25 points in Carousel for example.

How Ramio plays

  • 2 jokers or none: in a private room you choose; public games are played with jokers.
  • The joker stands for any card, in a run as in a set.
  • A meld can hold several jokers, as long as at least one real card remains.
  • On the table it can be moved and reused as often as you like, but it never goes back to a hand.
  • It doesn't count for the opening.
  • Left in hand at the end of a round, it costs 50 points.

Concrete cases, with Ramio's rule.

As soon as you have opened, in any meld: the joker takes whatever value is needed. A meld can even hold two, as long as one real card remains.

Can you move a joker that is already on the table?

Yes, and it is often the move that unlocks your turn. The usual way is to free the joker by putting a real card in its place, then take it where you need it.

The 9 of clubs takes the joker's place in the set. The joker moves to the run of hearts, where it is the 9, and opens the way for your 10: without moving it, the 10 of hearts had nowhere to go.

In Ramio, you don't even need the real card: a joker can leave any meld that still holds without it.

The run of spades stands without its joker. You take it and lay it with your queen and king of diamonds, where it is the ace or the jack. In Vatikan or Carousel, this move is not allowed.

Can you take the joker back into your hand?

This is the case that divides tables. On the table there is a run with a joker, and you hold the very card the joker stands for.

Table 5 JOKER 7
Hand 6
In Ramio 5 6 7 JOKER

Where taking it back is allowed, you put the 6 of diamonds in its place and the joker is yours again.

In Ramio, the 6 goes in its place but the joker stays on the table: in the same turn you move it into a valid meld, for example at the end of the same run (where it is the 8 of diamonds) or into another meld that needs it. It never goes back to your hand, even when you bring the card it stands for.

The same goes for a joker in a set. With the 9 of spades, the 9 of hearts and a joker on the table, your 9 of diamonds turns the set into a four, where the joker becomes the 9 of clubs. If someone brings the real 9 of clubs later, the joker has to move to another meld: a set never holds more than four cards.

How much does a joker left in hand cost?

In Ramio, 50 penalty points, more than any other card, in a custom game played to a points total (public games are a single round, without points): keeping it back for the perfect play is a risk. Other versions count it for less (25 points in Carousel): one more point to settle before you start.

One joker moved, one set repaired: the play goes through.

How do you play manipulation rummy with two players?

With two, the rules don't change: same melds, same shared table. What changes is the pace of the game.

  • Cards: Machiavelli and Vatikan deal 13 each, as Ramio does. Carousel for two uses a single deck with one joker and deals 10 cards each.
  • Stock: in Ramio, 26 cards are dealt and 80 stay in the stock (78 without jokers): up to 40 draws each, against 13 or 14 with four players.
  • Table: it fills up more slowly, since only two hands feed it. Early on there is little to take apart; then every meld your opponent lays down becomes material for you, and everything you lay down helps them on their next turn.
  • Drawing: with "draw only if you don't meld", a player who is stuck piles up cards while the other lightens their hand; with a draw every turn, as in Ramio, you both draw.

To play with two in Ramio, a private room is all you need: create it, send the six-character code and each of you plays on your own phone. On your own, there is the computer.

When does the game end, and how do you score?

Common rules

The round ends when a player lays down their last card: they win it. Scoring varies widely. Machiavelli and Shanghai count one penalty point per card left in hand; Finagle gives cards values (aces 15, tens and court cards 10, others 5); Vatikan has no scoring at all, the first player to go out wins.

How Ramio plays

Same ending: the round goes to whoever lays down their last card. The others count the penalties in the table below. Then it depends on the kind of game: points game or single round.

In Ramio, a game takes one of two forms:

  • Points game, set up as a custom game: penalties add up from round to round; the game ends as soon as a player reaches 100 points, and the player with the fewest wins. In a private room, the limit can be set from 50 to 300 points.
  • Single round: one round only, the first player to empty their hand wins. This is how public games are played.
  • If the stock runs out before anyone finishes, the round stops without a winner and each player counts the penalties in their own hand: whoever laid down well is rewarded, whoever held everything pays. In a single-round game, the round is replayed.

Manipulation rummy, classic rummy and tile rummy: the differences

Compared with classic rummy, where you draw, meld and discard:

  • no discard: you draw, you meld, that's all;
  • the table is shared and can be rebuilt: you play with the others' cards as much as with your own;
  • melds don't belong to anyone: what matters is that the whole table is valid at the end of your turn.

Compared with Rummikub, the best-known tile game of the same family, Ramio has:

  • cards instead of numbered tiles, with the usual suits and court cards;
  • an opening with a clean run of three instead of 30 points;
  • a joker that can be reused freely while it stays on the table;
  • in points games, a real penalty count (court cards 10, aces 11, joker 50) that turns every card you keep into a risk.

Frequently asked questions

How many cards do you deal in manipulation rummy?

It depends on the version: 13 each in Machiavelli and Vatikan, 10 in Carousel and Shanghai, 7 in Finagle. Ramio deals 13 to every player, from 2 to 4, with two 52-card decks: 104 cards, or 106 with 2 jokers.

Do you discard in manipulation rummy?

No. There is no discard pile: your hand only shrinks when you lay cards on the table. Ramio never discards either.

Do you draw a card every turn?

Not in most versions: in Machiavelli and Vatikan you either meld or draw one card, and in Shanghai and Finagle you draw only when you can't play. Ramio has you draw one card at the start of every turn.

Do you need a minimum to open?

Most versions have no opening requirement. Vatikan asks for a run of three in the same suit. Ramio asks for a clean run of three, with no joker and all from your hand, but no minimum score.

Can you take the joker back into your hand?

It depends on the table. In Carousel the freed joker must go straight into a new meld; some home rules let the player who brings the real card keep it. In Ramio, a joker can be moved on the table as often as you like during your turn, but it never goes back to a hand.

How much is a joker worth at the end of a round?

In Ramio, a joker left in hand costs 50 penalty points in a custom game played to a points total, the most expensive card ahead of the ace (11) and court cards (10); public games are a single round, without points. Other versions count it for less, 25 points in Carousel for example.

Can you play manipulation rummy with two players?

Yes. Machiavelli, Vatikan and Carousel can all be played by two. In Ramio the rules are the same as with three or four: 13 cards each, and a stock of 80 cards (78 without jokers).

Is manipulation rummy played with cards or tiles?

With cards: two standard 52-card decks. Rummikub, the best-known game of the family, uses numbered tiles instead, around the same idea of a shared table you rebuild. Ramio is the card version.

Is there a time limit per turn?

At a real table, the players decide. In Ramio, public games give 120 seconds per turn; in a private room, whoever creates the game chooses the time, or plays without a timer.

The same game in other countries

Manipulation rummy goes by other names elsewhere: in France it is called rami voleur, in Germany Räuber-Rommé, in Italy Machiavelli, in Brazil and Portugal mexe-mexe, in Hungary rablórömi and in Spain Maquiavelo; English Wikipedia describes it as Robbers' rummy. Everywhere the idea is the same: the table belongs to everyone and, on your turn, you can rearrange the melds already laid down. The linked pages explain the rules in each country's language.

The traditional versions quoted on this page (Carousel, Vatikan, Shanghai, Machiavelli, Finagle) are described in detail on pagat.com's Manipulation Rummy page, John McLeod's reference site for card game rules.
